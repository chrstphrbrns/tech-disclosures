**DEFENSIVE TECHNICAL DISCLOSURE**

**Systems and Methods for De Novo Automated Generation of Technical Concepts and Enabling Disclosures as Defensive Prior Art**

**Publication Date:** 2026-08-19 
**Disclosure Number:** CB-2026-0002  

---

### FIELD OF THE DISCLOSURE

This disclosure relates to automated generation of technical concepts and to systems and methods that invent, elaborate, and publish enabling technical disclosures de novo (without reliance on abstraction or translation of existing patent claims or other preexisting specific technical disclosures) for the purpose of establishing defensive prior art.

### BACKGROUND

The volume of potential patentable subject matter continues to expand rapidly across technological fields. Traditional defensive publication relies on manual authoring or on reworking of already-published material. Both approaches are limited in scale and foresight: they react to existing disclosures rather than proactively populating the prior-art landscape with novel concepts that have not yet been publicly articulated.

There is a need for automated systems and methods capable of generating technical architectures, systems, methods, and embodiments entirely de novo, expanding them into enabling disclosures, and publishing them with verifiable timestamps so as to dedicate those concepts to the public domain before others can claim them as patentable inventions.

### SUMMARY

Disclosed are systems, methods, and computer-readable media for the de novo automated generation of technical disclosures intended to serve as defensive prior art. In general terms, the invention comprises:

- automatically generating one or more technical concepts, architectures, systems, methods, or combinations thereof that are not derived by abstracting or translating any specific preexisting patent claim or equivalent detailed technical disclosure;
- elaborating each generated concept into one or more enabling technical disclosures containing sufficient detail for a person of ordinary skill in the relevant art(s) to practice the invention without undue experimentation;
- optionally producing variations, alternative embodiments, multi-domain realizations, or combinatorial expansions of the generated concepts;
- publishing the resulting disclosures in one or more publicly accessible venues with verifiable timestamps; and, optionally,
- monitoring later-published patent applications or claims for correspondence with the generated disclosures and generating alerts or submissions.

Generation of concepts may be performed by any suitable automated or semi-automated technique, including generative models, combinatorial search, evolutionary or optimization algorithms, knowledge-base synthesis, constraint-driven invention engines, multi-agent systems, simulation-guided discovery, or any combination thereof. The process does not require ingestion or generalization of existing patent claims or equivalent source disclosures as a starting point.

The disclosure is intended to place both the broad concept of de novo automated prior-art generation and numerous specific implementations into the public domain.

### DETAILED DESCRIPTION

#### General System

A computing system comprising one or more processors and one or more non-transitory computer-readable media stores instructions that, when executed, perform the steps described herein. The system may be realized as a single machine, distributed system, cloud service, edge deployment, or any other suitable architecture. Operation may be continuous, batch, real-time, scheduled, seeded by high-level goals, or fully autonomous.

#### Concept Generation (De Novo)

Technical concepts are generated without dependence on abstracting or domain-translating any specific preexisting patent publication, claim set, or equivalent detailed technical disclosure. Generation may begin from:

- high-level functional goals, problem statements, or constraint sets;
- libraries of known technical building blocks, primitives, or design patterns that are recombined in novel ways;
- statistical or neural generative models trained on broad technical corpora;
- evolutionary, genetic, or other search/optimization procedures that explore design spaces;
- multi-agent collaborative invention processes;
- simulation, reinforcement, or novelty-search driven discovery;
- random or structured sampling within defined technological spaces;
- or any other automated or hybrid computational method capable of producing new technical architectures or methods.

The output of this stage is one or more candidate technical concepts expressed at a level of abstraction suitable for further elaboration. Novelty relative to a reference corpus may optionally be assessed and used as a filter or ranking criterion, but is not required.

#### Elaboration into Enabling Disclosures

Each generated concept is expanded into one or more complete technical disclosures. Elaboration may include:

- detailed description of components, relationships, and operational flows;
- one or more concrete embodiments or use cases;
- alternative implementations, parameter ranges, or design choices;
- optional multi-domain realizations (applying the same core concept across different technological fields);
- diagrams, pseudocode, data structures, algorithms, or other supporting materials;
- statements of advantages, variations, and enablement information sufficient to satisfy applicable legal standards for prior art (e.g., enablement under 35 U.S.C. § 112(a) or equivalents).

Any suitable technique may be used for elaboration, including generative language or multimodal models, template-driven expansion, knowledge-graph instantiation, rule-based detailing, human-assisted refinement, or combinations thereof.

#### Optional Variation and Expansion

The system may automatically produce families of related disclosures by varying parameters, substituting components, combining concepts, exploring adjacent design spaces, or generating realizations in multiple technological domains. The degree of variation is configurable and may range from minor alternatives to substantially different embodiments of the same core idea.

#### Publication

Generated disclosures are published to one or more publicly accessible venues together with verifiable timestamps. Suitable venues include public websites or repositories with structured metadata, version-control systems, defensive-publication services, cryptographic or blockchain timestamping services, academic or technical archives, feeds, and any other medium that effectively places the material in the public domain or otherwise makes it available as prior art. Each publication preferably includes the full enabling text (and any accompanying materials), generation metadata, timestamps, and machine-readable identifiers.

#### Optional Monitoring and Response

Later-published patent applications, claims, or other technical documents may be monitored and compared against the corpus of generated disclosures. Detected correspondence may trigger alerts, internal records, or automated/semi-automated submissions to patent offices under applicable procedures.

#### Non-Limiting Implementation Details and Variations

Any of the foregoing steps may be performed in any order, combined, iterated, omitted, or executed in parallel. Preferred but non-limiting implementations employ large generative models (language, multimodal, or specialized technical models) guided by high-level prompts, constraints, or seed libraries, optionally augmented by search, evaluation, or multi-agent orchestration layers. Alternative implementations rely primarily on combinatorial algorithms, evolutionary computation, symbolic invention engines, simulation-based discovery, or hybrid pipelines that may include limited human oversight at any stage.

The system may generate disclosures focused on a single domain, spanning many domains, or deliberately targeting under-explored technological intersections. Output volume, depth of enablement, and novelty thresholds are fully configurable. The process is deliberately independent of any requirement to ingest, parse, abstract, or translate existing patent claims or equivalent specific source disclosures.

#### Illustrative Examples (Non-Limiting)

**Example A.** A generative system is seeded with high-level goals such as “resource-efficient coordination of heterogeneous actuators under uncertainty.” It produces a family of novel control architectures, elaborates each into detailed method and system disclosures with multiple embodiments, and publishes them with timestamps across several application domains (energy, robotics, logistics, etc.).

**Example B.** An evolutionary algorithm explores combinations of known sensing, decision, and actuation primitives under novelty and feasibility constraints. Promising individuals are automatically expanded into full enabling disclosures and published as prior art.

**Example C.** A multi-agent invention system iteratively proposes, critiques, and refines technical concepts in a target field (e.g., distributed sensing or adaptive materials). Finalized concepts are elaborated and published de novo.

Many additional generation strategies and output forms will be apparent to those of skill in the art.

#### Advantages

The disclosed systems and methods enable proactive, large-scale creation of enabling prior art that is not limited to concepts already present in the published literature. They can populate the public domain with novel technical ideas at a speed and volume unattainable by manual methods, thereby reducing the likelihood that later inventors can obtain patents on subject matter that has already been dedicated to the public.

### NOVEL ASPECTS DEDICATED TO THE PUBLIC

The following are dedicated to the public domain and are intended to serve as prior art against any later attempt to patent them:

- the general concept of automatically generating technical concepts or architectures de novo (without abstraction or translation of specific preexisting patent claims or equivalent detailed disclosures) and elaborating them into enabling technical disclosures for the purpose of creating defensive prior art;
- any system or method that performs automated or semi-automated de novo concept generation followed by automated or semi-automated elaboration into publishable enabling disclosures and public timestamped publication;
- use of generative models, combinatorial search, evolutionary algorithms, multi-agent systems, knowledge synthesis, simulation-guided discovery, constraint-driven invention, or any other computational technique for the de novo generation step;
- optional production of variations, multi-domain realizations, or combinatorial expansions;
- publication to any public venue or combination of venues with verifiable timestamps;
- optional monitoring of later claims and generation of alerts or patent-office submissions;
- all combinations, sub-combinations, and obvious variations of the foregoing.

Individual elements and sub-combinations are also dedicated to the public. The intent of this disclosure is to prevent the patenting of the described systems, methods, and concepts, whether claimed broadly or narrowly.
